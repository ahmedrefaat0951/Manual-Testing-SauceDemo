## Test Account

`standard_user`

## Preconditions

* User is logged in using the `standard_user` account.
* User has at least one product added to the Cart.

## Steps to Reproduce

1. Open the **Cart** page and verify that at least one product is present.
2. Click the **Checkout** button.
3. On the **Checkout: Your Information** page, click the **Cancel** button.
4. Observe the resulting page.
5. Return to the **Checkout: Your Information** page.
6. Enter valid customer information:
   * First Name: `Ahmed`
   * Last Name: `Refaat`
   * ZIP/Postal Code: `12345`
7. Click the **Continue** button.
8. On the **Checkout: Overview** page, click the **Cancel** button.
9. Observe the resulting page.
10. Compare the destinations reached by clicking **Cancel** on the two checkout pages.

## Expected Result

* The **Cancel** button should provide consistent navigation across the checkout pages.
* Clicking **Cancel** from both **Checkout: Your Information** and **Checkout: Overview** should redirect the user to the same destination.

## Actual Result

* Clicking the **Cancel** button on the **Checkout: Your Information** page redirected the user to the **Cart** page.
* Clicking the **Cancel** button on the **Checkout: Overview** page redirected the user to the **Products** page.

## User Impact

The inconsistent navigation may make the checkout flow less predictable for users. A user who becomes accustomed to being returned to one destination after clicking **Cancel** may find it confusing or frustrating when the same action on another checkout page redirects them to a different destination. This may make it more difficult for users to navigate the checkout flow confidently.
